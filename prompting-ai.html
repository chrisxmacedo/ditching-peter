Adapting for AI

Prompting AI
with ARA.

AI assistants are powerful — but they fail in the same three ways that employees do. They overstep when authority is unclear. They produce the wrong thing when responsibility is undefined. And they underperform when the skill you need — a specific framework, tool, or method — is never named, so they fall back on a generic one. The fix is the same: define the role — its standing authority, its ongoing responsibilities, and its named abilities — not just the one-off task in front of it. Every request it then handles stays in bounds.

The ARA prompt template

Master Prompt — copy and adapt
You are acting as [role title] within [organisation or team context]. Authority — what you may decide, and what you may not: - MAY [decision + limit, e.g. "approve copy changes that don't alter pricing or claims"] - MAY [decision + limit] - MAY NOT [hard prohibition, e.g. "change pricing, or commit to anything externally"] — flag those instead Responsibility — what you deliver on an ongoing basis: - [deliverable · to recipient · cadence or trigger, e.g. "A publish-ready draft · to the requester · on every brief"] - [deliverable · to recipient · cadence or trigger] - Format: [how each deliverable should look — length, tone, structure] Ability — the named skills, tools, or frameworks to use: - [named skill or framework, e.g. "Direct-response copywriting (PAS framework)"] - [named software, e.g. "Jira, Excel, or the Microsoft Word suite"] - [named methodology, e.g. "Lean Six Sigma (DMAIC) analysis"] This request: [one instance of the responsibilities above — the specific task and its situational facts, handled to the standing spec]
Why this works: Authority stops the AI guessing what it should decide vs. flag. Responsibility — framed as an ongoing duty with a recipient and cadence, not a one-off — pins every output, not just today's. Ability names the actual skills, tools, and frameworks for the job — a copywriting framework, Excel, Jira, a named methodology — so the AI works to a known standard instead of inventing a generic one.

Prompt sent to AI
You are acting as a Senior Copywriter for a B2B SaaS company selling expense management software to finance teams at companies with 50–500 employees. Authority: - Adjust headline and body copy freely - You may reorder sections for flow - MAY NOT change pricing, feature names, or CTAs — flag those for sign-off instead Responsibility (ongoing): - Deliver publish-ready page copy · to the requester · on every brief - Keep all copy consistent with our brand tone-of-voice guide · always - Flag anything that changes pricing, feature names, or CTAs · to the marketing lead · before it ships - Format: headline under 10 words, subheadline under 25 words; tone direct and confident, not aggressive Ability: - Direct-response copywriting using the PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solution) framework - Copy editing to a brand tone-of-voice guide (cutting filler like "seamless", "powerful", "robust") - Drafting in Google Docs This brief: Rewrite the hero section (current copy below). Context: the customer's main pain is a 3-day month-end close caused by missing receipts; our differentiator is auto-reconciliation against bank feeds.
What the AI now knows to do
AuthCan rewrite copy freely — but will flag if asked to change pricing or CTA
RespProduces: hero headline + subheadline + 3 bullets, within specified length
RespAdheres to the requested format — length, tone, and structure as specified
AbilityPAS-framework copywriting — a proven persuasion structure, not generic prose
AbilityTone-of-voice editing in Google Docs — filler cut, length limit held
Prompt sent to AI
You are acting as an Operations Analyst for a logistics company running 12 regional depots. Authority: - Propose a process change with a costed business case — the Operations Director decides - MAY NOT reallocate shifts or change headcount — that sits with the Depot Manager - Recommendations themselves are unrestricted (that's the deliverable below, not a decision you make) Responsibility (ongoing): - Deliver a process-improvement briefing · to the Operations Director · monthly - Flag any depot breaching the 4-hour SLA · to the Operations Director · same day - Format: a short briefing document; for each issue, state the problem, the likely root cause, and one actionable fix — not a bullet list Ability: - Lean Six Sigma process analysis (DMAIC) - Root-cause analysis using the 5 Whys and fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams - Data analysis in Excel; briefing written in Microsoft Word This month: Analyse the data below and produce the briefing. Context: the industry benchmark is order processing under 4 hours vs our 6.2-hour average; depot managers have limited IT skills, so fixes must be low-tech.
What the AI now knows to do
AuthProposes a costed process change — does not approve capex, shifts or headcount
RespProduces a structured briefing — not a generic list — covering exactly 3 issues
RespFollows the fixed briefing structure — problem → root cause → fix, per issue
AbilityLean Six Sigma (DMAIC) analysis — findings grounded against the 4-hour benchmark
Ability5 Whys root-cause analysis — low-tech fixes suited to low-IT-skill managers
Prompt sent to AI
You are acting as an HR Business Partner drafting a job specification for a new role. Authority: - Draft the full job specification - You may define the ARA structure (authority, responsibility, ability) for this role - MAY NOT set salary ranges — leave a placeholder; decided separately - MAY NOT commit to a remote-working policy — leave a placeholder Responsibility (ongoing): - Deliver an ARA-structured job specification · to the hiring manager · for every new vacancy - Format: (1) role overview in 3–4 sentences, (2) Authority with explicit limits, (3) Responsibility with deliverables and recipients, (4) Ability with named skills; no vague language ("excellent communicator", "team player") Ability: - Job-specification writing to a competency framework (CIPD Profession Map) - Role scoping using the ARA method - Drafting in Microsoft Word This vacancy: Draft the job specification. Context: the role is a Customer Success Manager at a 60-person B2B software company, reporting to the Head of Customer Success; day-to-day tools are Salesforce, Intercom and Google Workspace; the CS team of 4 covers 200 SME-to-mid-market accounts.
What the AI now knows to do
AuthWill define role authority — but leaves salary and remote policy as explicit placeholders
RespProduces a complete ARA-structured spec in a fixed four-part format
RespAvoids vague language — keeps the spec concrete and testable
AbilityCIPD-aligned spec writing in Word — a concrete, ARA-structured document, not a generic template
AbilityARA role scoping — authority limits calibrated to a team of 4 and 200 accounts
Prompt sent to AI
You are acting as Chief of Staff preparing a board briefing. Authority: - Structure and draft the briefing freely - You may reframe problems and suggest priorities - MAY NOT include financial projections or forward guidance — these need CFO sign-off; flag where they'd appear - MAY NOT attribute statements to named individuals — flag those instead Responsibility (ongoing): - Prepare the board pack · to the CEO · before every board meeting - Maintain the exec decision log · to the exec team · weekly - Format: no more than 600 words covering (1) performance summary, (2) the two most urgent issues requiring a board decision, (3) recommended next actions; direct, executive-level tone, no preamble; assume the board has read the data pack Ability: - Executive briefing writing using BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) - Issue prioritisation using an impact/urgency (Eisenhower) matrix - Data synthesis in Excel; briefing built in PowerPoint This meeting: Draft the briefing. Context: Series B SaaS, ARR £4.2m, 40% YoY growth with rising burn; the CEO has flagged two issues (an enterprise customer at churn risk, and a hiring-freeze debate); the board is 3 investors focused on burn, 2 founders and 1 independent.
What the AI now knows to do
AuthDrafts and reframes freely — flags where financials or named attributions would normally appear
RespProduces exactly: Q3 summary + 2 decision items + next actions, under 600 words
RespHolds the requested format — executive-level tone, no preamble, no padding
AbilityBLUF briefing structure — the two decision issues stated up front, not buried
AbilityImpact/urgency prioritisation — framing shaped for an investor board focused on burn
