Documentation · The ARA Role Spec

Every role,
defined as a spec.

A role isn't a process — the person holding it defines the process. ARA defines the envelope they work within: what they may decide, what they must deliver, and what they can do. Three declarative statements. One structured, readable, machine-legible spec per role.

01

May · Must · Can

One keyword per pillar. Each carries its own fields — the structure is identical for every role, so specs are comparable, diff-able, and auditable.

MAY

Authority

Permission · a decision

What the role is empowered to decide without asking. Bounded — every permission carries a limit and an escalation path.

MAY [decision]
  up to [limit]
  else [escalate to]
MUST

Responsibility

Obligation · a deliverable

What the role must produce, and for whom. A deliverable and a recipient — not an activity.

MUST [deliverable]
  to [recipient]
  when [cadence]
CAN

Ability

Capability · a skill

The named skills, tools, and frameworks the role must hold to do the above. Concrete, not aspirational.

CAN [named skill /
  tool / framework]
Declarative, not procedural. This says nothing about how the work flows — no steps, no sequence. It states what is true of the role. The person in it owns the process from there.

03

Why a spec, not prose

The same three statements, in the same shape, for every role — that structure is what makes ARA usable at scale.

01

Comparable

Every spec has the same fields, so you can diff two roles, spot an authority two people both claim, or a deliverable nobody owns.

02

Machine-legible

MAY / MUST / CAN with typed fields is data. An AI can audit it for gaps and overlaps, or act as the role — the same spec, read by a person or a model.

03

Handoffs are visible

One role's MUST … to → is the next role's input. Chain the recipients and the delivery process falls out of the specs for free.
